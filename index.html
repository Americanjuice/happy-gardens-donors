Our Story

Lydia's story

Lydia Awuor Onyango lost her mother when she was five and was later raised by her grandparents. As a child she took herself and her younger sister to school without books, pencils or uniforms. With the help of teachers, well-wishers and donors, she finished her education through college.

After 15 years teaching in Nairobi's slum settlements, she saw during COVID-19 how vulnerable children fell behind while others learned online. She began teaching children in her home with a cup of porridge, all she could afford. In 2021 she established Happy Garden Community School (HGCS) as a Day Care, responding to the need for accessible, affordable childcare and early learning. Its original program was a free education and feeding program.

As demand grew, the school expanded to pre-primary and lower primary. By 2026 it is a community-based school serving about 94 learners from Play Group and Day Care through Grade 4, with 11 teachers and supporting staff. Happy Garden Community School is a program of Happy Garden Community Organization (HGCO). It serves children from diverse backgrounds, including vulnerable and economically disadvantaged households and refugee families, and aims to give them a supportive environment where they can learn, eat, play, socialize and develop.

That growth has created a need for classrooms, learning resources, administrative and storage space, recreational facilities and other essential infrastructure. Lydia's goal is to reach at least 300 children and open more branches across Kenya.

“Feed a child, feed a community, and fuel them to school.” — Lydia Awuor Onyango

Registered as a Community Based Organization (CBO). Registration No. 03464. Government nonprofit letter available on request.

What We Do

Education, meals and care

Education for all

Play Group and Day Care through Grade 4. Every child is welcome, whatever their background or ability to pay fees.

Feeding program

Breakfast, lunch and snacks fight malnutrition. Some children come to school specifically for the meal. When friends sponsor food, Saturday meal days continue in the holidays.

Reading and writing

Children learn in English and Swahili. From January 2027, the school begins a free, paper-based writing pilot with Raven Ink to build comprehension.

Dedicated staff

11 teachers and supporting staff care for the children each day.
